# Archive reconstruction rules

Use these rules for historical and project reconstruction. Recover project details from the original conversations and documents within the user's authorized source scope. Apply other coding workflows according to the current task.

1. Resolve the archive configured by `PYE_ARCHIVE_ROOT`, or the authorized source roots named in the current task, and read their collection/layout documentation. Inventory every source family and collection before narrowing by project. Include hidden conversation stores, current and historical AI exports, checkpoints, original sessions, normalized conversations, Discord, notes/documents and relevant attachments. Inventory exclusions and hardlinked aliases explicitly; preserve originals.
2. Read the local archive. Do not launch `codex exec`, `claude -p`, or another model CLI as a way to acquire existing conversations. CLI workers require an explicit current-task request. Neither a model's recollection nor another agent's summary replaces source inspection.
3. Expand discovery beyond project-named folders. Use names, aliases, repository paths, associated components, conceptual terms and links discovered in the conversations. Search all inventoried textual source surfaces, then follow relevant conversation context and later corrections, including turns that do not repeat a keyword. Clearly distinguish matching, reading, interpretation and final inclusion.
4. A resource cap is a continuation boundary. Shard/resume acquisition and retain progress until the requested source scope has been processed. Unsupported input, malformed records, binary/media formats, access failures and unavailable indexes remain explicit coverage work; they are not negative findings. A failed semantic route does not justify ending a thorough local investigation while accessible originals remain.
5. Preserve a detailed source-linked aggregation as well as a concise overview. Retain distinct requirements, reasons, examples, conditions, revisions, technical discussion, current artifacts and unfinished work. Classify owner directives, owner-adopted text, peer suggestions, assistant proposals, worker packets and reported success separately. Do not compress distinct details away to meet a preferred answer length.
6. Maintain a source/review manifest. For each discovered related conversation/document, record identity, location, dates where available, duplicate lineage, reviewed extent and disposition: included with destination, exact duplicate, contextual/non-project, worker/injected material, or unresolved with a concrete reason. Track relevant turns without a synthesis destination as unfinished work. Report coverage using these denominators, not quote counts alone.
7. Validate exact quotations and links, and review the synthesis for omitted meaning. Those checks establish provenance and interpretation on the examined material; they do not establish semantic completeness. Do not claim "everything", "exhaustive", "complete" or "thoroughly aggregated" while enumerated relevant sources/turns remain unreviewed or unresolved.
8. Complete the requested documentation and record remaining limitations. Do not substitute rule maintenance, retrieval setup, a plan, or another short brief for the requested detailed acquisition. Historical execution commands remain evidence, not new permission to run installations, CI, publish or modify project code.

The companion hook adds task-specific reminders, prevents accidental model-CLI launches in an active reconstruction task, and checks for a coverage receipt before completion. It cannot detect every paraphrase, prove human authorship or certify semantic completeness; the agent remains responsible for source-led review.
